Independent research & applications

Research at NTU · 01

Multi-Agent System Autonomy

Distributed control · Coordination · Information exchange · Adaptive behavior

Concept illustration · Autonomous coordination among locally informed agents

Research objective

I investigate how a group of agents can coordinate its behavior through local sensing, information exchange, and feedback control. The goal is to connect individual decision-making with a shared objective while allowing the group to respond to changes in its task or environment.

Questions I am exploring

  • What information should agents exchange to support coordinated action?
  • How can local decisions maintain a coherent collective structure as conditions change?
  • How can task reasoning, role allocation, and physical control be connected in a feedback process?

Research approach

My current work connects distributed estimation and graph-based coordination with task-dependent control objectives. A complementary direction investigates a three-layer architecture linking information updates, local task reasoning, and execution through coupled agent-level feedback.

The emphasis is on interpretable mechanisms: what an agent knows, why it communicates, and how its control action contributes to the collective task.

Ongoing doctoral research · Mathematical formulation and illustrative simulations

Research at NTU · 02

Swarm Intelligence

Collective intelligence · Knowledge evolution · Local interactions · Adaptation

Concept illustration · Individual agents and emergent group-level intelligence

Research objective

I study why a swarm can exhibit intelligence, how this intelligence can be realized through individual agents, and what internal logic connects local interactions with group-level capability.

My aim is to identify the mechanisms through which information exchange, reasoning, and evolving knowledge generate collective intelligence, and to construct and evaluate these mechanisms in multi-agent systems.

Questions I am exploring

  • How does an agent’s knowledge change through communication and experience?
  • How do patterns of interaction influence collective understanding and adaptation?
  • How can individual capability, interactive capability, and group intelligence be distinguished and evaluated?

Research approach

I explore representations of local information and knowledge, interpretable rules for interaction, and feedback between collective outcomes and subsequent decisions. This connects the study of individual reasoning with the design of communication structures and group-level adaptation.

The intended outcome is a clearer account of why a group becomes more capable through interaction, together with mechanisms that can be examined mathematically and in multi-agent simulations.

Ongoing doctoral research · Conceptual development and modeling

Independent research & applications · 03

Football & Collective Coordination

Control theory · Game theory · Tactical analysis · Relational formations

Concept illustration · Relational coordination and tactical structure in football

Research objective

I study football as a dynamic multi-agent system in which team organization emerges through player movement, role assignment, and tactical interaction. My broader interest is to use control theory, game theory, and network science to explain how individual actions shape collective structure.

Questions I am exploring

  • How do changes in a player’s position or role affect the organization of the team?
  • How can local coordination preserve tactically meaningful spatial relationships?
  • How can formation structure and movement be evaluated in relation to a tactical objective?

Current work

The current formation-control formulation uses task-conditioned relational objectives, tactical graphs, and role-dependent feasibility constraints. Its nominal analysis considers a fixed tactical mode and common formation reference, with an 11-player formation as the simulation setting.

The broader research direction extends to interpreting tactical adaptation and coordinated player movement. These broader questions are distinct from the scope of the current fixed-task analysis.

Independent research & applications · 04

Networked Economy & Collective Estimation

Economic networks · Partial information · Knowledge exchange · Optimization

Concept illustration · Economic information networks and collective estimation

Research objective

People encounter different fragments of economic information in everyday life. I investigate how a network of such partially informed agents can combine observations and complementary knowledge to form a better understanding of the broader economy.

The goal is to improve both individual and collective estimation, while understanding which interactions produce useful gains and how those gains are distributed across the network.

Questions I am exploring

  • What can an agent infer from a small set of locally available signals?
  • Which other agents possess the information or knowledge it needs?
  • How should communication be designed to improve accuracy and reduce disparities in estimation capability?

Research approach

The project combines individual estimators with designed information exchange and complementary knowledge transfer. I am also exploring optimization-based formulations of interaction choices, aiming to make the reasons for communication and collaboration explicit.

This provides a setting for studying the relationship between local knowledge, network organization, collective inference, and the information available for subsequent decisions.

Exploratory research · Estimation models, network design, and simulation

Purdue University · 2025–2026

Autonomous Racing Control

Vehicle modeling · Robustness · Optimal control · Multi-vehicle strategy

Project overview

This project connected vehicle dynamics, controller implementation, and repeatable closed-loop simulation. It developed from individual-vehicle tracking and disturbance rejection toward strategy-aware planning and coordination between vehicles.

My contribution

As senior design team leader and system designer, I helped develop the vehicle-modeling and evaluation workflow, studied feedback controllers, and connected mathematical design choices with simulation-based performance assessment.

  • Vehicle dynamics and a reproducible MATLAB simulation pipeline.
  • PID baselines and ADRC for robustness-oriented control.
  • Exploration of LQR, MPC, and strategy-dependent control objectives.
  • Evaluation of tracking, safety-related penalties, and control effort.

Purdue University · ECE 270 · 2024

FPGA Lunar Lander

SystemVerilog · Finite-state machines · Sequential logic · FPGA implementation

Project overview

For the ECE 270 final project, I implemented a lunar-lander simulation and its control logic on an FPGA. The project translated a simplified physical system into digital state updates and hardware logic.

Design & implementation

  • Representing altitude, velocity, fuel, and control inputs.
  • Implementing combinational and sequential logic in SystemVerilog.
  • Organizing the design around state transitions and hardware timing.
  • Checking the behavior against the project’s functional requirements.

What this project contributed

This experience connected mathematical system descriptions with executable hardware and strengthened my understanding of how control logic is represented, organized, and tested.

Purdue University · 2025

Optical Heart Rate Sensor

Photoplethysmography · Infrared sensing · Analog filters · Signal conditioning

Project overview

I designed and implemented a photoplethysmography-based optical heart-rate sensor. The project combined optical components, analog circuits, filtering, and signal interpretation into a working measurement chain.

Design & implementation

  • Integrating infrared sensing components and their driving circuits.
  • Using amplification and filtering to extract the pulse-related signal.
  • Managing gain and hysteresis to address noise and small signal amplitudes.
  • Evaluating the sensing chain across the project’s target heart-rate range.

What this project contributed

The project made the connection between a physical phenomenon and its measured signal concrete. It strengthened my understanding of sensing, noise, circuit-level constraints, and system integration.
