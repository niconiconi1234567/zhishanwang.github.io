Information · Learning · Control

How local knowledge becomes collective action.

I study how agents with different observations, models and capabilities learn enough about their world to act together. My research connects the information a group possesses with the knowledge an individual can actually acquire—and the decisions that knowledge can support.

I am a PhD student in Electrical and Electronic Engineering at Nanyang Technological University, supervised by Prof. Rong Su at RIM Lab. I work across control theory, learning, information structure and strategic interaction, with applications ranging from robot teams to football tactics and economic networks.

Research question

OWN2 begins with partially informed estimators. OWN2 Plus asks the more fundamental question: which state distinctions can become available at all when sensing, local processing, communication, time and failure dependencies are constrained?

Approach

Describe the evidence that heterogeneous agents can generate through permitted operations and feasible protocols. Determine which hidden states remain observationally indistinguishable after the available evidence is combined. Choose capabilities and repeated measurements under finite resources and shared physical failure dependencies.

Current work

The program now separates two connected theory strands. One studies capability-constrained collective identifiability, same-episode information fusion and structural erasure resilience. The other studies failure-resilient information acquisition, robust coverage, finite-budget minimax risk and joint capability installation.

Scope & limits

Identifiability of a hidden state is distinct from identifying the communication graph itself. A rank condition must refer to information that is actually executable; an asymptotic error exponent is not the same objective as finite-budget error probability.

MISSION–CONTROL · Robotics · knowledge & execution

Mission Planning in Swarm Control Systems

Information, reasoning and execution inside each agent.

How can new evidence change what a robot should do next, and how can that decision remain connected to executable physical behavior?

A controller can track a reference without knowing whether that reference still serves the mission. This project asks how local observations and shared evidence can change rule support, available mission transitions and task choices, while keeping every decision connected to executable motion.

Rescue framework & three demos ↗

Core ideas

How the research is organized

01

Information

Collect local measurements, messages and observable teammate behavior with their source, time and object scope.

02

Task / reasoning

Check rule support, identify missing prerequisites and choose a justified task and mission transition.

03

Execution / control

Generate a reference, plan motion and enforce physical constraints; the resulting action changes the evidence available next.

Current progress

The research has progressed from a complementary-knowledge star prototype to three search-and-rescue demonstrations. Demo 0 activates and reuses evidence-supported transition rules; Demo 1 adds joint rescue prerequisites and physical evacuation; Demo 2 creates active information tasks and allocates heterogeneous sensing capabilities.

OGT1 · Game-theoretic information · causal protocols

Collective History Identifiability & Causal Attainability

Collectively available is not yet locally attainable.

A team may collectively hold enough evidence to distinguish two histories, yet no individual may be able to obtain that evidence in time.

This program starts from heterogeneous information sets in multi-agent interaction. It separates what all observations could reveal in principle from what an agent can learn through a causal communication and acquisition protocol.

Core ideas

How the research is organized

01

Structural distinction

Identify history pairs that different sensing capabilities can distinguish; characterize the remaining ambiguity.

02

Causal recovery

Construct admissible interaction and communication protocols that make the required distinctions available at the decision point.

03

Decision and control

Ask which distinctions are sufficient for an action, a functional observer or a stabilizing feedback law.

Current progress

The work has developed complementary structural and information-theoretic manuscripts: collective history identifiability and protocol synthesis; heterogeneous sensing with two-way communication; and extensions linking causal capability discovery to observer-based control and mean-square stabilizability.

Current theory manuscripts

Assumptions & scope

These are ongoing manuscript and theory developments. Their target venues are not presented as completed submissions. Collective access to all observations is not silently substituted for locally attainable knowledge.

Next questions

Study knowledge-gap discovery, interactive acquisition of complementary evidence and the network-dependent time and resource cost of obtaining control-relevant information.

Potential collaboration

A concrete place to begin.

Potential directions include causal protocol synthesis, network-dependent acquisition delays, and precise links between attainable information and closed-loop stability.

OWN · Learning & control · an outsider’s perspective

The Lonely Observer

Learn an unfamiliar system by observing its actions.

What can an outsider understand about an unfamiliar multi-agent system by observing what its members do?

The observer sees only parts of a group’s motion and executed actions, without direct access to its internal states, feedback laws or communications. The goal is to learn a useful model, understand the limits of that model and decide when the available understanding is sufficient to act.

Core ideas

How the research is organized

01

Level 1 · control value

Determine when observing actions improves a specified control task, and when finite data provide enough evidence to use a learned predictor.

02

Level 2 · distinguishability

Characterize the observation windows, network structure and action measurements needed to distinguish hidden feedback mechanisms.

03

Level 3 · participation

Study how an entering agent can learn while acting, and how information value interacts with probing costs and strategic response.

Current progress

The first two levels now have dedicated theory manuscripts. Level 1 separates richer dynamical information from actual control benefit. Level 2 studies finite-window identifiability, statistical reliability and action-sensing design. Level 3 is being developed through explicit models of participation under incomplete information.

Current theory manuscripts

LEVEL 1 · MANUSCRIPT IN PREPARATIONControl-Relevant Learning of Unknown Multi-Agent Systems through Action Observations: An Outsider's PerspectiveLEVEL 2 · MANUSCRIPT IN PREPARATIONHidden Feedback Distinguishability from Externally Observed Behavior: Observation-Window Limits, Statistical Reliability, and Action Sensing

Emerging extension · Game theory

Reciprocal strategic probing

What changes when an outside observer and an internal network know that they are observing one another? This developing direction studies two-sided signaling and active probing when both sides have private tasks, intentions or capabilities: each action can gather information, shape another agent’s inference and reveal something about its sender.

The aim is to connect dynamic signaling games with networked control and information limits, including how network and task conditions alter strategic disclosure and finite-sample identifiability. This is an emerging theory direction; equilibrium characterizations and identification bounds remain research objectives.

Assumptions & scope

Observing more hidden modes need not improve control. Passive prediction of existing behavior need not identify the response to a new intervention. The three levels retain these distinctions instead of treating learning, identification and decision quality as interchangeable.

Next questions

Develop decision-sufficient understanding and test how well behavior-based models transfer when the observer becomes a participant in the system.

Potential collaboration

A concrete place to begin.

Potential directions include finite-sample certificates for behavior-based prediction, action-sensing design, and strategic participation with only decision-sufficient knowledge.

BEHAVIOR INFERENCE · Multi-agent coordination · actions as observations

Learning from Teammates without Explicit Communication

Treat behavior as evidence, then test its decision value.

A teammate’s movement can reveal something about the world that a direct observation does not. Several movements together may reveal more than any one alone.

The project treats observed teammate actions as evidence about latent states and decision-relevant information. An agent combines this evidence with its own observations, updates its local mission and generates a physical control reference.

Core ideas

How the research is organized

01

Observe behavior

Read local motion or action histories without assuming access to a teammate’s internal state.

02

Infer useful structure

Distinguish hidden possibilities using behavioral relations; account for ambiguity and the information already available.

03

Adapt a local mission

Use the inferred information to revise a task, role or reference and evaluate the resulting closed-loop behavior.

Current progress

A submitted ISCS 2027 manuscript connects a general observation–inference–mission–control loop with football-inspired simulations. The studies examine complementary behavior information and explicitly compare inference against stronger position-based alternatives.

Related papers

Assumptions & scope

The value of behavior inference depends on what the baseline already observes. A benefit over delayed reaction does not establish a benefit over every position-only controller.

Next questions

Extend toward changing local goals, robust uncertainty handling and complementary role coordination without explicit messages.

Potential collaboration

A concrete place to begin.

Potential directions include observation-matched benchmarks, uncertainty-aware teammate models, and complementary robot roles inferred from behavior.

OWN2 · Economic estimation · heterogeneous information

From private cognition to collective estimation

Different observations. Different models. A shared estimation problem.

Each agent sees only part of the world and reasons with an imperfect private model. OWN2 asks how interaction can improve what the agents understand without assuming a central observer who already knows everything.

Macroeconomic estimation provides a concrete testbed: 30 heterogeneous agents use subsets of 22 signals to estimate inflation. The broader research question concerns how private evidence becomes a belief, how beliefs should be interpreted, and which information is worth using or forwarding.

Core ideas

How the research is organized

01

Private cognition

An agent forms an estimate from its own signals and local model. Partial information and imperfect reasoning remain explicit.

02

Networked consultation

Exchange beliefs using confidence informed by historical reliability, while retaining private information sets and models.

03

Interpret · use · forward

Separate local estimation from recipient-specific communication. Select information for the ambiguity it can resolve, rather than merely average every message.

SWARM INTELLIGENCE · Knowledge · interaction · emergent capability

When interaction creates a collective capability

A collection of agents is not yet an intelligent collective.

My interest in swarm intelligence centers on the mechanism by which local observations and interactions produce capabilities that no agent possesses alone.

What knowledge can an agent form, which missing evidence can it obtain from another, and how does the resulting understanding support a new action? This viewpoint connects the engineering of robot teams to mathematical questions of identifiability, knowledge acquisition and learning.

Core ideas

How the research is organized

01

Form knowledge

Relate local measurements and reasoning capabilities to the evidence an agent can produce.

02

Acquire what is missing

Use interaction to discover and obtain complementary evidence under network and resource constraints.

03

Revise behavior

Use supported knowledge to update a task, repair an execution path or change a control decision.

Current progress

The work connects the rescue demonstrations to OWN2 Plus information foundations, OGT1 causal attainability and the behavior-learning questions of The Lonely Observer.

Assumptions & scope

The current rescue demonstrations activate and compose specified candidate rules. Learning the rule representation itself is a distinct next theoretical question, not an ability already established by those demonstrations.

Next questions

Formalize knowledge and rule learning, including what constitutes a new rule, how evidence supports it, and how its use changes reachable behavior.

Potential collaboration

A concrete place to begin.

← Mission Planning in Swarm Control Systems

RIM Lab · Research demonstrations

Learning rules. Revising missions. Acting together.

Evidence-driven mission planning and control for multi-robot rescue. The central question is how a robot can turn incomplete local knowledge into a justified mission decision—and carry that decision through to physical execution.

New information should change what the team can responsibly do next.

A rescue instruction is not enough by itself. A robot must establish that a target is a person, that the person can follow, that a route is suitable, and that the necessary rule and coordination conditions hold. The project connects these questions inside each agent through an Information–Reasoning–Execution loop.

The research architecture

Three layers, one closed loop.

From information to executable missions

How evidence opens the next step.

The sequence below is a reading guide to a coupled process. Evidence is checked, confidence is revised, and missing information can send the agent back to sensing. A newly supported rule can enable a transition or task that was previously unavailable; it does not create an arbitrary new controller.

01

Observe with provenance

Local sensing and messages enter with their source, time and object scope. Relaying the same observation must not create a second independent piece of evidence.

02

Assess confidence

Fact confidence asks whether a proposition is supported. Rule support asks whether a candidate transition rule is justified for use. The two quantities have different meanings.

03

Find the missing evidence

When a mission prerequisite is unresolved, the system can form an information-gathering task: inspect a candidate, check a route or obtain a complementary observation.

04

Activate a supported rule

The available rule template becomes usable only after its support and grounding conditions hold. Repeated messages alone do not supply unlimited support.

05

Extend the executable mission

Update the available task and transition choices, checking the relevant facts, rule guards and cooperation conditions. This expands what the agent can execute within the specified mission structure.

06

Execute and observe again

Map the selected task to a path, reference and control action. Physical progress and new observations feed the next reasoning update; a failed guard can require a safe hold or repair.

A useful distinction in Demo 2

Facts use evidence accumulated under the stated observation model. Rule applicability uses a bounded support score with local grounding requirements. That rule score is not a Bayesian probability. The demonstration learns support for supplied rule templates; it does not claim unrestricted rule invention.

The demonstrations connect local information, rule support, mission decisions and continuous motion within supplied scenarios. Task-Conditioned Distributed Relational Formation Control Under a Global Formation Objective

Zhishan Wang and Rong Su

ICARCV 2026 · Accepted conference paper

Read manuscript PDF ↗Related research →

The formation’s shape and its collective placement are distinct control objectives.

Relational formationDistributed estimationTask-conditioned control

Research overview

This work studies task-conditioned formation control for a team of locally interacting agents. A global formation objective specifies the reference centroid, orientation and scale, while relational constraints describe the internal geometry. The framework separates the tactical interaction graph from the communication graph and couples distributed estimation of the global objective with local feedback. An 11-agent football-inspired simulation illustrates formation recovery and centroid tracking under the stated sensing, graph and controller assumptions.

A visual reading guide

The mechanism at a glance

01

Describe the task

Specify the collective reference through centroid, orientation and scale, rather than prescribe unrelated trajectories for every agent.

02

Separate two networks

Use a tactical graph to represent relational constraints and a communication graph to distribute information about the common objective.

03

Close the loop

Combine local estimates and relational feedback so internal shape and collective placement can be analyzed together.

Figures from the research

Evidence in the manuscript

The numerical example examines an 11-agent formation and reports the evolution of formation and centroid-estimation errors. It illustrates the theoretical mechanism under nominal simulation conditions.

How to interpret the result

The analytical guarantees depend on the stated graph, sensing and gain assumptions. The football example supports a formation-control result; it is not a model of a complete competitive match.

Questions this opens

Extend the formulation to changing objectives, role adaptation and behavior-derived information while preserving the distinction between sensing, communication and control.

How to Interpret, What to Use, and What to Forward: Decentralized Information Interpretation and Selection for Multi-Agent Global-State Estimation

Zhishan Wang, Zhengyang Liu, and Rong Su

AAMAS 2027 · Research Paper Track

Request manuscript ↗Related research →

What helps my estimate and what helps my neighbor need not be the same packet.

Decentralized estimationInformation selectionBelief packetsSubmodular coverageMulti-hop communication

Abstract

We study decentralized information selection in multi-agent estimation with heterogeneous partial information, imperfect local reasoning, and limited communication. We separate local use from recipient-specific forwarding and represent beliefs as immutable packets that may propagate over multiple hops. Each agent evaluates packets by their ability to resolve remaining state ambiguity, yielding a monotone submodular weighted-coverage objective with local Greedy guarantees. Online likelihood adaptation further refines how agents interpret received beliefs. Synthetic and real-data experiments show that selective communication improves estimation over private reasoning, with multi-hop relay and adaptive interpretation contributing substantially to the gains. A macroeconomic case study with 30 agents and 22 distributed signals demonstrates historical U.S. inflation estimation and illustrates how heterogeneous local information can be interpreted, selected, and propagated toward a shared global objective without centralized aggregation. The framework also provides a general information-processing structure with potential applications to social, economic, swarm, and other multi-agent networks under local communication constraints.

A visual reading guide

The mechanism at a glance

01

Preserve the evidence

Represent a belief as an immutable packet with source information, so forwarding does not turn copies into new independent evidence.

02

Separate use from forwarding

An agent can forward evidence that is valuable to a neighbor even when it adds little to its own estimate.

03

Select by remaining ambiguity

A weighted-coverage formulation gives local greedy selection guarantees, while online likelihood adaptation improves packet interpretation.

Figures from the research

Evidence in the manuscript

The manuscript combines an analytic example, controlled synthetic studies and a historical U.S. inflation case with 30 agents and 22 distributed signals. The experimental design isolates multi-hop relay, adaptive interpretation and selective communication through matched baselines and ablations.

How to interpret the result

The greedy guarantee concerns the stated coverage objective, not a universal guarantee of lower realized estimation error. Statistical interpretation depends on the likelihood model and the available evidence. This is a submitted manuscript, not an accepted publication.

Questions this opens

Study task-dependent information value, capability constraints and the relationship between selected communication and downstream decision quality.

Inferring Hidden States and Decision-Relevant Information from Neighboring Agents’ Actions in Multi-Agent Systems

Zhishan Wang

2027 SICE International Symposium on Control Systems (ISCS)

Read manuscript PDF ↗Related research →

Two individually ambiguous behaviors can become informative when considered together.

Multi-agent systemsBehavior-mediated coordinationHidden-state inferenceDistributed controlFootball tactics

Abstract

Multi-agent coordination often relies on explicit communication, yet agents also reveal task-relevant information through their physical behavior. This paper studies whether relations among multiple observed behaviors can reveal information that is unavailable from each behavior alone, and whether such information can improve the observer’s own decision. We formulate a behavior-mediated coordination framework that connects local observation, joint inference, mission/role selection, and physical execution. In a football-inspired model, we show that two individually uninformative motions can become jointly informative about a latent tactical opportunity, and derive conditions under which this information changes the observer’s mission from waiting to active coverage. Closed-loop simulations show that the resulting decision gain can persist under physical dynamics. A ten-agent formation study further shows that motion-based reasoning improves over delayed reaction, while providing no additional benefit over a strong position-only baseline in the tested settings. These results distinguish behavioral identifiability, decision value, and closed-loop coordination benefit.

A visual reading guide

The mechanism at a glance

01

Relations carry information

Two motion observations may each be uninformative about a hidden opportunity while their relation distinguishes it.

02

Information changes a mission

The inferred opportunity affects the choice between waiting and active coverage under an explicit decision criterion.

03

Close the inference–control loop

Evaluate whether the decision advantage survives physical dynamics, and compare it with strong observation-matched baselines.

Figures from the research

Evidence in the manuscript

A football-inspired study links joint behavioral inference to WAIT-to-COVER decisions and physical execution. A ten-agent formation experiment provides a useful boundary: motion-based reasoning improves over delayed reaction, but brings no additional benefit over the strong position-only baseline in the tested settings.

How to interpret the result

No explicit runtime messages are exchanged for the coordination decisions studied. Shared prior structure and local behavior models may still be available. The result is about the specified models and tested baselines, not a general claim that observing actions always improves teamwork.

Questions this opens

Connect behavior inference to unknown feedback models, changing missions and decision-sufficient understanding in unfamiliar teams.

From Private Cognition to Collective Estimation: A Confidence-Aware Networked Consultation Framework for Heterogeneous Agents with Partial Information and Partial Reasoning

Zhishan Wang and Rong Su

ICSC · Submitted

Read manuscript PDF ↗Related research →

Belief exchange can change an estimate while the private reasoning model stays fixed.

Partial informationPartial reasoningConfidence-aware consultationDistributed estimation

Research overview

This work studies collective estimation when every agent has only partial information and an imperfect private reasoning map. Each agent first learns a local estimator and evaluates its historical reliability. Agents then exchange belief-level information over a communication graph and update their current estimates through confidence-weighted consultation. The local information sets and private reasoning maps remain fixed during consultation. A U.S. inflation testbed examines how communication changes individual and population-level estimation across heterogeneous agents and network structures.

A visual reading guide

The mechanism at a glance

01

Private cognition

Each agent learns an estimator from its own subset of signals, preserving heterogeneity rather than assuming a common complete model.

02

Reliability-aware exchange

Historical estimation performance informs the confidence attached to a neighbor’s belief.

03

Controlled comparison

Compare private and consulted estimates across communication topologies while keeping private information and reasoning maps fixed.

Inside the method

What each agent knows

Each agent receives five signals from a balanced 22-signal pool and learns a private 5–8–1 feedforward estimator. Equal information capacity and model architecture do not eliminate differences in realized estimation quality.

What crosses the network

Agents share estimates with performance-informed confidence. Consultation operates on beliefs; the private signal set and learned reasoning map stay fixed. This isolates the value of social exchange from model retraining.

How the comparison is read

The study distinguishes mean individual error from the error of the population-mean estimate. Four fixed graph structures are compared, followed by a historical out-of-sample check. The results concern the evaluated U.S. inflation dataset and settings.

Figures from the research

Evidence in the manuscript

The manuscript evaluates 30 heterogeneous agents, each using five of 22 economic signals, across four fixed communication structures. Under AuthorityHub, mean individual normalized RMSE decreases from 0.8212 to 0.4443 over 2015–2017. In the historical out-of-sample period, 2018–2019, the corresponding values are 0.8350 and 0.4000. The figures below show how belief consultation changes the population of estimates without retraining the private models.

How to interpret the result

This paper studies belief-level consultation. The broader OWN2 platform also explores knowledge assimilation; changing a current estimate is not the same as learning a new private reasoning map.

Questions this opens

Move from fixed consultation structures toward adaptive information interpretation and selective forwarding, as developed in the related AAMAS submission.

Translating Team Strategy into Closed-Loop Vehicle Control for Multi-Agent Autonomous Racing

Zhishan Wang, Kaijie Zhu, Zhengyang Liu, Luhang Hong, Zheng Qing, Jacob Junjie Zhang, and Rong Su

ICSC · Submitted

Read manuscript PDF ↗Related research →

Consensus on shared intent should preserve the differences that make team roles useful.

Autonomous racingRole-aware coordinationStrategy-to-control mappingClosed-loop execution

Research overview

The project develops a role-aware strategy-to-control framework for multi-agent autonomous racing. A common team strategy is combined with vehicle-specific role offsets. Distributed coordination acts on role-normalized residuals, aligning shared intent while preserving functional differences between vehicles. An explicit interface maps the resulting strategy to references, cooperative behavior and controller parameters. The analysis and simulations distinguish agreement on team intent from the feasibility of downstream planning and physical execution.

A visual reading guide

The mechanism at a glance

01

Preserve role differences

Coordinate the common part of a strategy instead of forcing vehicles with different roles to adopt identical behavior.

02

Make the interface explicit

Map high-level objectives to local references, cooperation settings and controller parameters.

03

Diagnose the execution chain

Separate information access, coordination, planning feasibility and physical tracking when interpreting a team outcome.

Inside the method

Normalize before coordinating

A vehicle’s desired strategy combines a shared team component with a role offset. Coordination acts on the residual after that offset is removed, so agreement does not erase the distinction between Lead and Support.

Translate strategy into execution

The interface maps the coordinated strategy to desired motion, cooperation behavior, controller weights and clearance preference. The architecture makes the influence of high-level intent inspectable at the reference and control layers.

Separate recovery from feasibility

Restricted target access tests whether the intended strategy can propagate through neighbors. Planning-horizon and lane-spacing experiments then ask whether the downstream vehicle system can execute it. Solving the first problem does not automatically solve the second.

Figures from the research

Evidence in the manuscript

Controlled simulations examine the strategy-to-execution mapping, differentiated Lead–Support behavior and the role of neighbor coordination when access to the target is restricted. They also expose cases where strategy agreement does not resolve a downstream planning limitation.

How to interpret the result

The analytical convergence statement concerns the anchored coordination mechanism under its stated connectivity, pinning and spectral conditions. It is not a blanket guarantee of safe or successful vehicle execution.

Questions this opens

Develop interfaces that connect team strategy, feasible planning and vehicle dynamics with explicit guarantees across the full execution chain.

Purdue University · 2025

Optical Heart Rate Sensor

Photoplethysmography · Infrared sensing · Analog filters · Signal conditioning

Project overview

I designed and implemented a photoplethysmography-based optical heart-rate sensor. The project combined optical components, analog circuits, filtering, and signal interpretation into a working measurement chain.

Design & implementation

  • Integrating infrared sensing components and their driving circuits.
  • Using amplification and filtering to extract the pulse-related signal.
  • Managing gain and hysteresis to address noise and small signal amplitudes.
  • Evaluating the sensing chain across the project’s target heart-rate range.

What this project contributed

The project made the connection between a physical phenomenon and its measured signal concrete. It strengthened my understanding of sensing, noise, circuit-level constraints, and system integration.
